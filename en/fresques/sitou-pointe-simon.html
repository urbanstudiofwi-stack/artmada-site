The story of the mural

At the corner of rue du Commerce and boulevard Saint-Simon, look up: a monumental portrait by Togolese artist Sitou, one of the pioneers of street art in West Africa, whose murals draw on his travels and African roots.

A Black woman in profile, head slightly raised, eyes closed, lost in thought. Her face is broken into pastel geometric shapes, like a mosaic; her blue headscarf carries a tiare flower and a monstera leaf, a nod to Caribbean nature.

From her ear hangs the Sankofa symbol: in Akan culture (Ghana) it means “go back to the source”, learning from the past to move forward. A large orange circle behind her reads as a sun or a halo. The mural pays tribute to the bond between Africa and the Caribbean: separated by the sea, united by shared roots.

Text : Digital Street Art (Creapolis trail commissioned by ARTMADA)

The artist

Sitou

Originally from Togo, Sitou started painting graffiti in 2003 and became one of West Africa's street art pioneers. A committed artist, he uses Afro-urban culture to address major social issues. The chameleon, his totem animal, stands for adapting within diversity.
